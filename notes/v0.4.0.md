## A 3D view of your ride

osscycler now has a 3D view: ride the road itself, behind your rider or
through their eyes, with your numbers above it, the grade of the next
250 m and the course's profile below. It runs on a Linux PC with a
graphics card (a TV driven by a PC works well) and holds 60 frames a
second on a mid-range one. The terminal view is still there, for a
Raspberry Pi or over SSH, and both can be open on one ride.

Start it with one command:

```sh
osscycler
```

It uses the core already running on this computer, or starts one for as
long as the screen is open, and opens the 3D view where it's installed
and there is a display, else the terminal view. `osscycler -fake` tries
it all without a trainer.

Everything the terminal view does, the 3D view does by keyboard: the
menu, courses, history and activities, workouts and their editor, fixed
power, grade or resistance, your profile, calibration, and arranging the
numbers on screen (`o`, `z`).

## A world for every course

Every course gets a 3D world of its own, built from OpenStreetMap and,
in the Netherlands, the national height model (AHN):

- the roads it rides and the roads around it, with junctions,
  roundabouts, sidewalks and cycle lanes;
- the land along it: fields, meadows, woods, heath, water, towns, with
  trees by species and age, bushes, hedges, grass and heather; poplars
  along country roads and pollard willows along ditches; woods by the
  sea stay low;
- the buildings, with their roofs.

The Posbank Loop and the two test tracks come ready-built. Any other
course is built on your own computer the first time you ride it: you
ride a preview right away, the screen says what's being built and about
how long it will take, and the world fades in when it's ready. Worlds
of routes that share roads are the same where they meet.

## Smaller things

- `p`, `P` or space pause and carry on. Pausing holds the clocks and
  sets the trainer flat; a ride no screen is watching is paused after
  30 seconds and ended (and saved) after 30 minutes.
- Your profile is in the menu now.
- `q` opens the menu; its Quit asks, with a ride under way, whether to
  end and save it or leave it paused.
- The map data credit is shown in the start menu.

## Installing

The packages now contain `osscycler`, the 3D view, the terminal view,
the core and the world builder, with the ready-built worlds; they are
larger (about 125 MB) because the 3D view brings its own engine and
.NET runtime. The macOS download has the terminal view and the world
builder; the 3D view is Linux only for now. The licences of the
software and data osscycler includes are in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.
