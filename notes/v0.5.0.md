## Laps, and riding a course the other way

A course that comes back to where it started is now ridden as laps: the
road from the start to the loop is a run-in, then you go round and
round until you stop, each lap timed, with your best lap on the course
as the ghost. The Posbank Loop is ridden this way: the run-in from
Rheden to the Diepesteeg, then the lap.

Every course can be ridden the other way too: "Posbank Loop (reverse)"
is in the course list beside the Posbank Loop, a course of its own with
its own best times, ghosts and history. The test tracks have reverses
as well.

Changed your mind halfway? Press `r` twice during a ride to turn round:
the ride so far doesn't count, and you ride on the other way from the
same spot, untimed until you cross a timing line (on a lapped course,
the lap's line starts a timed lap). On a run-in, before the lap, you
can't turn round; the screen says so.

A one-way course ends at its line, with a summary of the ride.

## After the ride, and during it

- A summary at the end of a ride and for any recorded activity
  (`i` in ACTIVITIES): averages, normalized power, your power curve from
  5 seconds to an hour, and time in power and heart rate zones.
- A graph of power and heart rate over the whole activity, shown as you
  ride (`t` shows or hides it).
- Heart rate zones come from a maximum heart rate you can set in your
  profile; osscycler shows the highest you have held for 10 seconds in
  your rides, but never fills it in for you.
- In workouts, the cadence tile says whether you're on an interval's
  target cadence.
- The 3D view has a map of the course in a corner (it's one of the
  strips `o` arranges).

## No trainer to hand

At the start, osscycler now says what is missing (the ANT+ stick, the
trainer, its power or cadence, a heart rate strap) and lets you carry on
without it, or with a simulated rider in the trainer's place (`s`), to
try things out. Nothing is recorded while simulated.

## The worlds

- Worlds are now built road network first: the junctions, the roads
  between them, then what lies beside the roads, and the land last,
  eased into the roads' edges. Rides are smoother, with fewer steps,
  holes and kinks, through junctions and roundabouts most of all.
- Farmland is sown: ploughed, or under maize, wheat or potatoes, with a
  grass headland along the road. Meadows are fenced where no ditch is,
  with gates and field entrances. Towns have their own street trees.
- France: worlds in France are built from IGN's height model (RGE ALTI)
  and buildings (BD TOPO), in a French style: woods by altitude, from
  oak and beech in the valleys to stunted beech on the crests, guardrails
  where the land falls away, stone retaining walls, snow poles and
  kilometre markers, French houses and farms, and distant mountains out
  to 5 km under a light haze.
- A new included route: the La Bresse – Hohneck loop in the Vosges,
  34.7 km and 828 m of climbing, up the Route des Américains to the
  Hohneck and back. Its world is built on your computer the first time
  you ride it.
- The sun stands where it would for the place and the day, now and then
  low in the morning or evening.

Worlds you built with 0.4.0 are rebuilt once, the first time you ride
them; the ready-built Posbank Loop and test tracks come new.

## The 3D view

- Worlds of the courses in your list are built ahead while you aren't
  riding, from the map data already on your computer (the profile
  chooses whether that happens during rides too, and whether data may be
  downloaded for it).
- Sharper on large screens: 3D is drawn at up to 1440 lines and scaled
  up.
- Roads, walls and rock have surface detail; buildings have windows and
  doors; bushes cast shadows.

## Smaller things

- The grade colours turn yellow at 6 % (it was 9 %): 6 % is already
  hefty.
- `esc` opens the menu during a ride even when it's already paused.
- In the terminal view, the number you type replaces a suggested value
  (typing 5 after a suggested 3 % gave 35).
- The ghost rides at its own recorded pace between updates, no longer
  jumpy.

## Installing

As before: the Linux packages and archives contain `osscycler`, the 3D
view, the terminal view, the core and the world builder, with the
ready-built worlds; the macOS download has the terminal view and the
world builder. The licences of the software and data osscycler includes
are in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.
