## Smoother, truer worlds

- Roundabouts on a hillside lie nearly level, as real ones are graded:
  the ring tilts no more than 4 %, and the roads coming in ease onto
  it. Before, a ring could follow a steep slope, so you went up and down
  metres within a quarter of it, and its asphalt could cover part of the
  island.
- Bridges over a road are built as skew bridges: their ends, the
  concrete they rest on and the expansion joint across the road run
  parallel to the road below. Square ends left a corner of the support
  hanging in the air, and its top could show through the road.
- A long bridge's piers stand in the median and beside the hard
  shoulders, never in the lanes of the road beneath.
- The Posbank Loop's cycle underpass under the N348 is built as a real
  one: room for service vehicles (2.7 m under the deck) and for two
  cycle paths, its slopes paved with grey concrete blocks as one smooth
  plane on each side, under both carriageways and through the open strip
  between them.
- Motorways near a course are drawn, with their markings, barriers and
  a clear verge. Where a course passes under one it goes through a
  tunnel with lit concrete walls, and where it crosses over one, over a
  bridge. Roads crossing on different levels are kept apart in height.
- The Posbank Loop comes back along the Hoofdstraat at Rheden station,
  the through road, instead of jogging through the Stationsweg. Riding
  it the other way no longer takes a sharp zigzag there.
- Drains, snow poles, bend posts and kilometre markers along French
  roads keep their places when the map round them changes.
- A world built while the map data server was down (some of the map
  missing) is built again, with the map, the next time you ride it,
  instead of staying without roads where data was missing.
- The figure 8 test track has a village with a T-junction, a
  roundabout, a car park and a river bridge, and a motorway it passes
  under and over. Where it crosses itself, the passes are 4 m apart
  rather than 10, over a gentle grass dike.

Worlds you built with 0.5.0 are rebuilt once, the first time you ride
them; the ready-built Posbank Loop and test tracks come new.

## The 3D view

- The course list says which worlds are ready to ride, and which will
  be built when you first ride them (their map data isn't on your
  computer yet).
- Grass beside the road no longer reaches out over it, where it could
  stand in front of the ghost riding ahead of you.

## Heart rate zones

- If you know your threshold heart rate rather than your maximum, set
  that in your profile: the zones then come from it (five zones after
  Joe Friel). With both set, you choose which the zones use.

## Smaller things

- Practising a section of a lapped course (the core's `-ride-start-m`)
  starts where you ask, untimed until you cross the lap's line; it used
  to put you back at the lap's start.
- In the terminal view, `esc` or `q` on a paused ride opens the menu and
  keeps it open; it used to close again at once.
- In the terminal view's road view, side roads follow their own course
  rather than a straight line, so they no longer cross buildings.

## Installing

As before: the Linux packages and archives contain `osscycler`, the 3D
view, the terminal view, the core and the world builder, with the
ready-built worlds; the macOS download has the terminal view and the
world builder. The licences of the software and data osscycler includes
are in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.
